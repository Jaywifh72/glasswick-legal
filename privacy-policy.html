Privacy Policy for Glasswick

Last updated: 15 November 2026 (America/Toronto).

Glasswick is the current name of this game. A French version of this policy will be prepared later.

Who we are

ChickaPoppy is the studio that makes Glasswick, a stained-glass logic puzzle game for iPhones, iPads and Android phones. The studio is based in Pointe-Claire, Québec, Canada.

The person responsible for personal information (the privacy officer under Québec's Law 25) is Jay Boileau.

If you have a question about this policy or about your information, contact:

  • Email: jeanjacquesboileau@gmail.com
  • Mail: ChickaPoppy, Jay Boileau, 212 boul. Saint-Jean, Pointe-Claire QC H9S 4Z3, Canada

What this policy covers

This policy explains what information the mobile game collects, how it is used, and what rights you have. It covers the store builds of the game (the versions with ads, optional purchases, analytics and crash reporting). The free web build of the game has no ads and no purchases, and it does not talk to our servers.

The game is meant for a mixed audience: it is a puzzle game for a general audience, and that audience includes children. We treat anyone under 14, and anyone whose age we do not yet know, as a child for privacy and advertising, in every country.

No accounts, and your progress stays on your device

The game does not have accounts or a login. There is no ChickaPoppy server that stores your progress.

Your windows, tools, settings and purchase records are saved on your device. If you delete the game, or if you get a new phone and do not restore a backup, that save can be lost. (The Android store build also turns off automatic backup of this save, so a device restore will not bring back spent tools.)

We do not sell your information, and we do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising.

Information the game uses

Date of birth (age screen)

The store build will ask you to enter your date of birth so the game can show an experience that fits your age (for example, no ads for children in Canada, child-safe ads where ads are shown, and no tracking of children). That screen lets you type the date freely. It is not pre-filled to a particular age.

The date of birth is kept on your device so the game can remember your choice. It is not sent to a ChickaPoppy server, and it is not sent to Google Analytics, Firebase Crashlytics, AdMob or RevenueCat. Anyone under 14, or anyone who has not yet entered a date, is treated as a child. The game starts with its most private settings already on (measurement off, no advertising identifiers, and, in Canada, no ads on the child path) so you do not have to change a setting first.

Advertising (Google AdMob)

The store build can show ads between windows and optional rewarded ads that you choose to watch. Ads are provided by Google AdMob, part of Google LLC.

In Canada, children and anyone whose age we do not yet know are not shown ads, and they are not shown commercial offers during play. If a parent approves a purchase, it happens only in the shop. We take this approach in light of Québec's Consumer Protection Act, sections 248 and 249 (commercial advertising directed at persons under thirteen). Players who are 14 or over in Canada may still be shown ads.

Google's advertising systems may process information such as:

  • your device's IP address, which can be used to estimate a general location
  • how you interact with the app and with ads (for example launches, taps, and video views)
  • diagnostic information about the app and the ads SDK
  • device identifiers, including the Android advertising ID or Apple's Identifier for Advertisers (IDFA), only when the player is 14 or over and (on iPhone or iPad) only if tracking permission is granted

Google's published disclosures:

  • Android / Play Data safety: https://developers.google.com/admob/android/privacy/play-data-disclosure
  • iOS / App Store nutrition labels: https://developers.google.com/admob/ios/privacy/data-disclosure
  • Google's advertising policy: https://policies.google.com/technologies/ads
  • Google Privacy Policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy

Outside Canada, for children and for anyone whose age we do not yet know:

  • Ads are non-personalized / contextual only. We do not use interest-based advertising or remarketing for these players.
  • We do not use the Android advertising ID (AAID) or Apple's IDFA to track or personalize ads for these players.
  • We ask Google to treat those ad requests as child-directed (or the equivalent current "child" age-treatment setting) and to cap ad content at a "G" rating.
  • We do not show Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt to these players, because we will not track them.

Those child-safe ad rules, where ads are shown, follow Google Play's Families policy and AdMob's Families guidance:

  • https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335
  • https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6223431
  • https://developers.google.com/admob/android/targeting

Consent messages (Google User Messaging Platform)

In regions where the law requires it (including the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, and some U.S. state regimes), the game uses Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP) to show a consent message before personalized ads start, for players who are 14 or over. You can open privacy options again later from Settings, when the law requires that entry point.

For children and for anyone under the age of consent, we tell UMP not to request consent for ads personalization. UMP does not automatically pass that setting to the ads SDK, so the game also sets child age treatment on the ad requests themselves. See https://developers.google.com/admob/android/privacy/gdpr.

Analytics (Google Analytics for Firebase)

The store build uses Google Analytics for Firebase, in a Firebase project that is used only for this game, so we can see how the game is played (for example, how many people open it, which screens they visit, and which purchases succeed). The exact events are usage and diagnostic events, not your name or your date of birth.

When this is collected:

  • Nothing is collected until you have entered a date of birth on the age screen.
  • If you are 14 or over, collection is on. We show a short notice the first time, and you can turn this off or back on in Settings at any time. Turning it off is not locked or hidden behind a parent check or any other gate.
  • If you are under 14, or if we do not yet know your age, collection stays off. There is no parent setting that turns it on.

When collection is on, Analytics may collect:

  • an app-instance identifier (not your name, and not linked to a ChickaPoppy account — there is none)
  • approximate location at country level (we turn off Google's "granular location and device data" setting, so city-level location and device model are not collected for Analytics)
  • app activity such as screen views, sessions, and (when you buy something) in-app purchase events

We do not collect advertising identifiers for Analytics, for anyone: no Android advertising ID, no Apple IDFA, and no Identifier for Vendor (IDFV). The iOS Analytics package is installed without the capability that would collect IDFA. We do not set a user id, and we do not set Analytics user properties. We turn off advertising-personalization signals, we use Google's consent mode so ad storage, ad user data and ad personalization stay denied, we keep Google signals off, and we do not turn on Analytics data sharing with Google products and services. Data retention is set to two months, and "reset on new activity" is off so identifiers can expire.

Official controls and disclosures:

  • Configure collection (iOS): https://firebase.google.com/docs/analytics/ios/configure-data-collection
  • Consent mode for apps: https://developers.google.com/tag-platform/security/guides/app-consent
  • Play Data safety: https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/11582702
  • Apple privacy labels: https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/10285841
  • Data retention: https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/7667196
  • Google signals: https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9445345
  • Granular location and device data: https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/12002752
  • Data sharing settings: https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/1011397 and https://support.google.com/firebase/answer/6383877

Crash reporting (Firebase Crashlytics)

The store build uses Firebase Crashlytics so we can find and fix crashes. It follows the same on/off rules as Analytics: off until the age screen has an answer; on for players 14 or over (first-launch notice and a Settings toggle; turning it off is not gated); off, always, for under-14 players and for anyone whose age we do not yet know. There is no parent setting that turns it on for a child.

When collection is on, Crashlytics may collect:

  • stack traces and the state of the app at the time of a crash
  • device and operating-system information used to debug the crash
  • a Crashlytics installation identifier, used to count how many people are affected by a crash
  • a Firebase installation ID (used only to rotate that Crashlytics identifier)
  • session information used for quality metrics in the Firebase console

We do not attach your name, your date of birth, or a ChickaPoppy account id to crash reports (there is no account). We do not use Crashlytics for advertising.

If collection is off, Crashlytics may still store a crash report on the device. We will not later enable collection on an install that belongs to a child or to anyone whose age we do not know, so those stored reports are not uploaded.

Official disclosures and controls:

  • Play Data safety: https://firebase.google.com/docs/android/play-data-disclosure
  • Apple privacy labels: https://firebase.google.com/docs/ios/app-store-data-collection
  • Opt-in / collection flags: https://firebase.google.com/docs/crashlytics/customize-crash-reports
  • Retention (crash traces and identifiers kept for 90 days): https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy

Feature flags and A/B tests (Firebase Remote Config, planned)

We plan to use Firebase Remote Config for simple A/B tests (a randomly chosen variant, the same for a given install, with no personalization). We will not use Remote Config personalization, and we will not target experiments using user properties, advertising ids, or your age.

Remote Config, when it is added, will follow the same on/off rules as Analytics. Official documentation says it may collect country, language, time zone, app and device version information, and a Firebase installation id. See https://firebase.google.com/docs/android/play-data-disclosure and https://firebase.google.com/docs/ios/app-store-data-collection. This policy will be updated before a build that includes Remote Config ships.

Purchases (RevenueCat and the official stores)

Optional purchases go through Apple or Google. We use RevenueCat to confirm what you bought so the game can unlock it on your device. Children may buy any item in the shop, including Plus subscriptions and the lifetime pack, but only after a parent or guardian confirms the purchase in the game. Auto-renewing subscriptions are presented as subscriptions, with the price, the billing period, and how to cancel, before that confirmation. Apple's Ask to Buy and Google Play family payment controls may also apply.

RevenueCat's published disclosures say it collects purchase history, and that this collection is required for the purchase feature and for RevenueCat's own analytics of purchases. It does not collect payment card numbers. See:

  • https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/platform-resources/apple-platform-resources/apple-app-privacy
  • https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/platform-resources/google-platform-resources/google-plays-data-safety
  • https://www.revenuecat.com/privacy

The game does not ask you to create a RevenueCat account. We do not start RevenueCat, and we do not create an anonymous RevenueCat app user id, until a purchase is actually started. For a child, that is only after a parent or guardian confirms. The identifier is used to restore a purchase, not to profile you. We do not send advertising identifiers to RevenueCat, and we do not turn on RevenueCat advertising or attribution integrations.

Daily reminder (local notifications)

You can turn on an optional daily reminder after your first daily window, and you can change or turn it off in Settings. The reminder is scheduled on your device by the system notification service. It does not go through a ChickaPoppy server. The game plans up to three days of reminders ahead and then stays quiet.

The game asks the phone for notification permission the first time, and a "no" is respected. For players treated as children, a parent or guardian must confirm before reminders are turned on.

Information we do not collect

The game does not have:

  • an account or profile
  • chat, friends, or user-generated content
  • access to your precise location, contacts, photos, microphone or camera
  • advertising identifiers in Analytics (no AAID, IDFA or IDFV), for anyone
  • Analytics user properties or a user id
  • analytics, crash reporting, or Remote Config fetches from children or from anyone whose age we do not know

Children

We do not knowingly collect personal information from children to build marketing profiles, and we do not use children's information for interest-based advertising. In Canada, children and anyone whose age we do not yet know are not shown ads or in-play commercial offers. Where ads are shown to children outside Canada, they are contextual only, with no advertising identifiers. Analytics and Crashlytics stay off for children, and there is no parent setting that turns them on. We do not collect advertising identifiers or set user properties for anyone in Analytics.

Because the game includes children in its audience, we apply children's privacy rules, including the U.S. Children's Online Privacy Protection Rule (COPPA), as amended in 2025. The amended Rule is in force; operators had until 22 April 2026 to comply with most of the 2025 changes, which is before this game's planned launch. See 16 C.F.R. Part 312, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312, and the official final rule at https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule.

COPPA generally requires a clear notice and verifiable parental consent before collecting personal information from a child under 13, unless a narrow exception applies. "Personal information" includes persistent identifiers such as a device id or an IP address. For a mixed-audience service, COPPA allows a neutral age check first. We will not collect other personal information before that age check, other than as those limited exceptions allow. As a conservative default, we treat under 14 (not only under 13) as children in every region, including for Analytics and Crashlytics.

Canadian and Québec rules also apply. The studio is in Québec, and the first launch is in Canada:

  • Québec's Law 25, which amended the *Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector* (P-39.1). Section 4.1 says personal information about a minor under 14 may not be collected from that minor without the consent of the person having parental authority or of the tutor, unless collecting the information is clearly for the minor's benefit: https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/version/lc/P-39.1?code=se:4_1. Section 9.1 says that when a technological product or service has privacy settings, those settings must provide the highest level of confidentiality by default, without any intervention by the person concerned: https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/version/lc/P-39.1?code=se:9_1. The Commission d'accès à l'information also summarises these changes: https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/protection-renseignements-personnels/sujets-et-domaines-dinteret/principaux-changements-loi-25.
  • Canada's PIPEDA and the Office of the Privacy Commissioner's Children's Privacy Code: https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/ and https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-for-businesses/protection-of-children-for-businesses/childrens-privacy-code/children-code-final-report-2026.

How long we keep information

Save data and any date of birth kept for the age screen stay on your device until you delete the game or clear that data.

Purchase records needed to restore what you bought are kept by Apple, Google and RevenueCat for as long as needed to provide the purchase and to meet their legal duties. We do not keep a separate ChickaPoppy copy on our own servers today.

Analytics user-level data is set to the shortest retention the property allows (2 months), and "reset on new activity" is off so identifiers can expire. Crashlytics keeps crash traces and related identifiers for 90 days, then starts removing them, according to Google's documentation.

We do not keep children's personal information indefinitely (COPPA § 312.10). We do not hold an Analytics or Crashlytics profile on Google's servers for a child, or for anyone whose age we do not know.

Your rights

Your rights depend on where you live. We will not refuse a sincere request because it arrived from a particular country, but the legal test may differ. Email jeanjacquesboileau@gmail.com.

Canada (PIPEDA) and Québec (Law 25). You can ask whether we hold personal information about you, see it, correct it, and in many cases have it deleted. Under Law 25 you may also have a right to data portability (to receive computerized personal information that was collected from you in a commonly used structured format) and, in some situations, a right to de-indexation. You may withdraw consent, subject to the law and to any information we must keep. You can complain to us first, and then to the Commission d'accès à l'information du Québec (https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/) or the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada (https://www.priv.gc.ca/).

European Economic Area and United Kingdom (GDPR / UK GDPR). If we process your personal data, you may have rights to access, correction, deletion, restriction, objection, and data portability, and the right to withdraw consent. For information-society services offered to a child, GDPR Article 8 sets the age of consent at 16 unless a Member State has set a lower age no younger than 13. We still treat under 14 as children for our own age screen. The UK Age Appropriate Design Code also applies to online services likely to be accessed by people under 18 in the UK: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/. You may complain to your local data protection authority, including the ICO in the UK.

California (CCPA / CPRA). We do not sell personal information and we do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising. You may request access, correction or deletion of personal information we hold. We will not discriminate against you for exercising these rights. We do not sell or share children's information for those purposes.

Because most game data lives only on your device, the most direct way to delete it is to delete the app or clear the app's storage. For information held by Google, Apple or RevenueCat, we will tell you how to reach them or we will pass your request on where we can.

Security

We do not run a game backend today. Information that leaves the device (ads, analytics and crash reports for players 14 and over, and purchase receipts) is sent to those providers over HTTPS/TLS, according to their documentation. You should still use a device lock and keep the operating system up to date.

If we have a confidentiality incident that involves personal information and presents a serious risk of harm, Québec law requires us to notify the Commission d'accès à l'information and the people affected.

International transfers

ChickaPoppy is in Québec. Google, Apple and RevenueCat may process information in the United States and in other countries.
